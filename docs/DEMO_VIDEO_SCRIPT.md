## How to actually sound human (the part that fixes "robotic")

The voiceover above is already conversational. To deliver it that way:

1. **Read each shot out loud, alone, 5 times.** First two times will sound stiff. By the fifth, you'll find your rhythm.
2. **Don't memorize.** Memorize the *meaning* of each beat, then say it in your own words on the day. If "Fatema's been waiting too long" doesn't feel right, say "Fatema's been waiting forever". Your version is fine.
3. **Pauses.** A 1-second silence after a strong line is the most underrated tool. Use them.
4. **Smile when you mean to sound warm.** A smile changes your voice, even on audio.
5. **Sit forward.** Don't slouch. Voice carries energy from posture.
6. **One pass per shot.** Record each shot's voiceover separately. If a line is bad, redo just that line, not the whole thing.
7. **Use contractions.** "I am" → "I'm". "She is" → "She's". "Did not" → "Didn't". Native speakers do.
8. **Cut every "very", "really", "incredibly", "powerful".** If you remove them, the line is stronger.

## Recording flow (one evening, 3 hours total)

### Hour 1: Setup
- Install OBS, set 1080p 60fps
- Hide Chrome bookmarks, zoom 100%, incognito
- Pre-process one case so localhost has finished cascade ready
- Gather B-roll images, dump in a folder
- Test record 30 seconds, watch back, fix audio

### Hour 2: Record
- Pass A: silent screen recording of Shots 3, 4, 5, 6
- Pass B: voiceover (each shot in separate file in Audacity)
- Re-record any line that stumbles

### Hour 3: Edit in DaVinci Resolve
- Drop screen footage on V1, voice on A1, B-roll images on V2
- Cut to fit the timestamps
- Speed up Shot 5 middle at 6x
- Add quiet music (-22dB) on A2
- Add English subtitles burned in
- Export 1080p, H.264, 12 Mbps, ~150 MB
